Part II · A week of tracked eBay listings

Plated in Gold

From asking to selling: which Apple Watches sell, for how much, and how fast

Part I studied asking prices in a one-time snapshot of web-scraped listings, and found that aftermarket 24k gold plating made older models look more valuable than the newest Ultra. Part II tracks what actually sells: a collector polled the eBay Browse API for a week, following US Apple Watch listings from first sighting to their end, and recorded completed sales.

How to read this: sale prices are the final auction bid or the listed price of a fixed-price listing whose sold counter went up. Accepted Best Offers are recorded at the listed price, so fixed-price sale prices are an upper bound. The collection window is one week, with of downtime.

How the data was collected

    API calls and listing observations per hour, Mountain Time. Shaded spans are collection gaps; no data was recorded in them. Spikes are the daily full sweeps.

    Limitations

      Future work

      • Run the collector for several months without gaps, to measure seasonality and estimate time to sale for slow, high-priced listings, including the gold-plated ones.
      • Track Best Offer acceptance directly, so fixed-price sale prices are exact rather than an upper bound.
      • Store the buying options seen on each observation, so an auction's Best Offer and Buy It Now settings can be used as features without leaking the outcome.
      • Add other marketplaces and compare clearing prices across them, closing the loop with Part I's multi-site snapshot.
